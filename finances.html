Send this to Claude or your accountant
The file carries every line the bank showed, so it still adds up to your statement — and it opens with a plain-English note explaining what a transfer, a directors loan and your own drawings each mean, and which of them count. That note is the point: without it you get asked about the same lines one at a time.
Set a category and it is saved straight away — and remembered, so the same merchant is filled in automatically from now on. A credit that is not really income — money in from one of your own accounts, or a directors loan you will pay back — gets that answer in the Category column instead, and then it counts nowhere. The ✕ on the right deletes a row outright: it stops counting, it stops being offered, and it does not come back on the next import. Nothing is thrown away — the Deleted chip above lists them and puts any of them back.

Last six months
Profit First
Cash status reads green once the month is this far in front, amber while it is between zero and there, and red the moment spending is ahead of income. The pots are taken off card takings only — Stripe and GoCardless — not off loans, refunds or transfers. The week runs from the day above to the day before it; the closing day is left out on purpose, because its payments are still landing, and it counts in the following week instead.
Income sources
Used to split income by source, and to work out the meal-side profit (SumUp taken, less what went out to Nutraprep). "Own accounts" is how a move to or from your tax and investment pots is recognised: those rows are marked Transfer and left out of every total, because they are your money changing pocket, not spending. Put as many names in that box as you like, separated by commas — whatever your tax pot, your savings and your cards are called on the statement. Anything the box misses you can still set by hand on the Transactions tab: every credit there can be marked Transfer or Directors loan, and either one is left out of income.
